우리 같이 웃었던 날, 영화를 틀어놓고

  • 우리 we (inf.)
    • 저희 we (f.)
  • 같이 together
  • 웃다 to laugh
  • 날 day
  • 영화 movie
  • N~을/를 object particle
    • 을 attaches to a word ending in a consonant, 를 to words ending in vowels. This particle is used to mark the object of a verb.
    • ex. 사과를 좋아해요. [I] like apples.
  • 틀다 to turn; to turn on (video, movie)
  • A/V~고 and
    • 고 attaches to verbs and adjectives to list them, essentially meaning "and."
    • ex. 이 음식이 맛있고 싸다. This food is delicious and cheap.

서로만 봤던 날, 그날

  • 서로 each other
  • ~만 only
    • 만, meaning "only," attaches to nouns.
    • 한국어만 공부해요. I only study Korean.
  • 보다 to see, look at
  • 이 N, 그 N, 저 N this, that N
    • 이 means "this."
    • 그 means "that." It is used to talk about something closer to the listener than the speaker.
    • 저 means "that (over there)." It is used to talk about something far from the listener and speaker.
    • All three must be followed by a noun.

솔직히 말해 그냥 아무런

  • 말하다 to speak
    • 말씀하다 (hon.)
  • 그냥 just

이유 없지 사랑에 대하여

  • 이유 reason
  • 없다 to not be, exist
    • ant. 있다 to be, exist
    • Though they are both adjectives, 있다 and 없다 conjugate like verbs.
  • A/V~지(요)
    • ~지(요) in a statement adds a feeling of confirmation or something like "of course."
    • ex. 청소했지요. Of course, I cleaned.
  • 사랑 love

어리고 작은 이 맘에 넘쳐

  • 어리다 to be young (childish)
    • ant. 늙다 to be old
    • syn. 젊다 to be young (youthful)
  • 작다 to be small
    • 크다 to be big
  • A~은
    • 은 attaches to an adjective stem to modify a noun.
    • ex. 차가 작아요. The car is small.
    • ex. 작은 차가 있어요. I have a small car.
  • N~이/가 subject particle
    • 이 attaches to nouns ending in consonants, and 가 to those ending in vowels. It is used to mark the subject of a verb or adjective.
    • ex. 이 아이돌이 유명해요. This idol is famous.
  • 맘 heart, short for 마음
  • N~에 in
    • ~에 attaches to nouns to mean something is happening "in" that noun.
    • ex. 도서관은 서울에 있어요. The library is in Seoul.

흘러버린 이 많은 건 love

  • 흐르다 to flow
  • 많다 many
  • 거/것 thing
    • Both 거 and 것 mean the same thing. In this case, 건 is a contraction of 것은.

자라나 버린 아이가 되어

  • 아이 child
    • alternative forms: 애, 아가 (baby)
  • 되다 to become
    • You will see this verb conjugated as both 되어 and 돼.

네 곁에서 이 밤을 지새워

  • 네 your (short for 너의)
    • 저의 also becomes 제, and 나의 becomes 내.
    • To differentiate 내 and 네 in speech, 네 is often pronounced 니.
  • N~에서 in/at N
    • 에서 is different from 에 because it is used with actions. N에 goes with a stative verb, like 있다. N에서 is used with action verbs.
    • ex. 학교에서 점심을 먹었어요. I ate lunch at school.
  • 밤 night

너의 작은 미소 안에 번지, 아리따운 이 놀이에서 너와 평생 빠져나가기 싫어

  • 미소 smile
  • 안 inside
  • 놀이 game
  • N와/과 and, with
    • 을/를, 이/가, 은/는, etc., 와 comes after words ending in vowels, while 과 comes after consonants.
    • This is a formal way to say "and" or "with."
    • ex. 한국과 영국의 문화 차이에 대해 말했어요. I spoke about the differences between Korean and English culture.
  • 빠지다 to fall into
  • 나가다 to leave
  • 빠져나가다 to fall out of
  • V기 nominalising particle
    • ~기 attaches to verbs to make them into nouns, just as gerunds (-ing words) do in English.
    • It is generally used with well-known activities rather than niche ones, and it is used in a few common grammar structures, such as ~기 싫다 (to hate doing.)
  • 싫다 to be unwilling, to be distasteful
    • ant. 좋다
    • Both 싫다 and 좋다, as with other adjectives, can be added to ~아/어하다 to make them verbs.
    • ex. 나는 딸기가 싫다. I dislike strawberries.

내 평생의 반을 네게로

  • 내 my
  • 반 half
  • N에게로
    • 에게로 is a compound of ~에게 (to) and ~로 (with) to mark the direct target of an action.
    • Here, it is 너에게로 contracted to 네게로 meaning "to you (directly)."

너의 꽃이 몇백만 번쯤 또 시들어도

  • 꽃 flower
  • 몇 some (number of)
  • 백 hundred
  • 만 ten thousand
  • 번 frequency counter
  • ~쯤 approximately
    • 몇 and 번 are best explained in context.
    • 몇 should be used in the same places a number can be used, when the number is unknown but not too big.
    • 번 must be attached to some kind of numeral, including 몇, to denote a number in sequence or an amount in frequency.
    • ex. 1번의 답은 A이다. The answer to number 1 is A.
    • Here, 몇백만 번쯤 means "some millions of times."
  • 또 again (once again)
    • syn. 다시 again
    • While 또 means again in a repeated sense, 다시 means again as in starting over.
    • ex. 또 보자! Let's meet again!
    • ex. 다시 말해 주세요. Please say that again.

새로운 향을 다시 머금고

  • 새롭다 to be new
  • 다시 again

더 찬란한 꽃을 네 맘에 피워 줄게

  • 더 more
  • 꽃 flower
  • 피우다 to bloom
  • V~아/어 주다 to do V for (someone)
    • This pattern is used to ask or offer something for someone.
    • ex. 빵을 데워 주세요. Please warm the bread.
    • ex. 방 청소해 줄게. I'll clean your room.
  • V~ㄹ게(요) offering to do something for someone
    • ~ㄹ게요 marks that the speaker is going to do an action unless the listener objects.

    • ex. 설렘 (설레다), 이름 (이르다), 웃음 (웃다), etc.
