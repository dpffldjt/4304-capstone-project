Employee: 저희가 사실... 30분 뒤에 미팅이 있어가지고

  • 저희 we (formal)
    • syn. 우리 we (informal)
  • N~이/가 subject particle
    • 이 attaches to nouns ending in consonants, and 가 to those ending in vowels. It is used to mark the subject of a verb or adjective.
    • ex. 이 아이돌이 유명해요. This idol is famous.
  • 사실 truth
  • 분 minutes counter
    • Minutes are read in Sino-Korean numbers (30분 = 삼십분)
    • The number before is usually written in numerals, but it can be written in 한글.
  • 뒤 behind, after
  • N~에 in
    • ~에 attaches to nouns to mean something is happening "in" that noun.
    • ex. 도서관은 서울에 있어요. The library is in Seoul.
  • 미팅 meeting
    • syn. 회의 meeting
  • 있다 to be, exist
    • ant. 없다 to not be, exist
    • Though they are both adjectives, 있다 and 없다 conjugate like verbs.

Employee: 저희 6명에 맞춰서 6부 프린트해 주실 수 있으실지

  • 명 counter for people
    • This counter is read with native Korean numbers (ex. 6명 = 여섯명)
    • syn. 분 (formal). #분 cannot be used when including the speaker.
  • 맞추다 to meet, match
  • V~아/어 주다 to do V for (someone)
    • This pattern is used to ask or offer something for someone.
    • ex. 빵을 데워 주세요. Please warm the bread.
    • ex. 방 청소해 줄게. I'll clean your room.
  • A/V~시 honorific marker
    • This is added to any verb or adjective stem whose subject should be honored. Even when speaking with informal language, if the subject is honorable, 시 is added before any other verb endings.
    • ex. 선생님이 바쁘셔. Teacher is busy.
  • V~ㄹ 수 있다 to be able to do
    • ant. V~ㄹ 수 없다 to not be able to do

Wooyoung: 짬 때리시는 거 잖아요?

  • V~는 것 nominaliser
    • This structure takes verbs and adjectives and lets you talk about them as nouns.
    • For adjectives, us A~ㄴ 것
    • ex. 내 아이돌이 대상을 받는 것 원해요. I want my idol to win a daesang.
  • ~잖다? Isn't it?
    • This is a contraction of ~지 않다.
    • As a question, X잖아요? means "It isn't X, is it?" As a statement, X잖아요 means "Of course, it's X."

Employee: 산 님이 프린트해 주시고

  • ~님 honorific attached to names and titles
    • For names, 씨 has historically been more common, ex. 지민 씨
    • The MZ generation has tended toward 님 instead, which has historically mostly been used with titles, like 선생님, 고객님, 사장님, etc.
  • A/V~고 and
    • 고 attaches to verbs and adjectives to list them, essentially meaning "and."
    • ex. 이 음식이 맛있고 싸다. This food is delicious and cheap.

Caption: [이미 알아서 진행 중]

  • 이미 already (unsurprised)
    • syn. 벌써 "already" implies something was unexpected to the speaker.
  • 알다 to know
    • ant. 모르다 to not know
  • A/V~아/어서 so
    • This is used to show cause and effect. Its main use is to show a reason for the second clause.
    • ex. 배고파서 먹어요. I am hungry, so I eat.
    • It can also be used to show more indirect cause and effect, like two actions that happen in sequence.
    • ex. 학교에 가서 공부했어요. I went to school and studied.
  • 중 in the middle of
    • N 중

Employee: 스캔 업무를 해주시면 좋을 거 같은데

  • 스캔 scan
  • N~을/를 object particle
    • 을 attaches to a word ending in a consonant, 를 to words ending in vowels. This particle is used to mark the object of a verb.
    • ex. 사과를 좋아해요. [I] like apples.
  • A/V면 if
    • A면 B = If A, B
    • ~면 좋다 means "It would be good if..."
  • ~ㄹ 것 같다 It seems like
    • This is used to make commands/requests less direct, especially in office settings.
  • A(으)ㄴ데/V는데/N인데 but
    • While it means "but," it can also be used to soften statements.
    • It can also end sentences with ㄴ/는데요.

Employee: 그래서 이 계약서 저기... 기계 가서 스캔해 주시면 될 거 같고

  • 그래서 so
  • 이 N, 그 N, 저 N this, that N
    • 이 means "this."
    • 그 means "that." It is used to talk about something closer to the listener than the speaker.
    • 저 means "that (over there)." It is used to talk about something far from the listener and speaker.
    • All three must be followed by a noun.
  • 저기 (over) there
    • Parallel to 이/그/저, there are 3 distinct place-denoting nouns, unlike English's two.
    • 이 -> 여기 here
    • 그 -> 거기 there (by the listener)
    • 저 -> 저기 over there
  • 가다 to go

Wooyoung: 몇 장이요?

  • 몇 some (number of)
    • 몇 should be used in the same places as numbers can to denote an unknown quantity.
  • 장 counter for wide, flat things
    • 장 is used with native Korean numbers.
    • It counts wide, flat objects like paper, towels, trash bags, and glass.
  • N~(이)요
    • (이)요 ends statements and questions politely without a verb or adjective.
    • 이요 attaches to nouns ending in consonants, and 요 to those ending in vowels.

Employee: 아 스캔해서 저한테 이메일로 보내주시면 되는데

  • 저 I, me (polite)
    • syn. 나 (informal)
  • ~한테 to/from
    • 한테서 can be used to specify "from," but it can typically be understood from context.
    • 에게(서) is used in the same way formally and in writing.
  • 이메일 email
  • ~(으)로 via, with
    • ~(으)로 marks the direct target of the action.
  • 보내다 to send
  • ~면 되다 All you need to do is...
    • As above, 면 means "if," so with 되다 (to become, to amount to) it literally means "if you do X, it will amount to Y."
    • ex. 여권 있으면 돼요. All you need is a passport.

Wooyoung: 아 그게 무슨 말이에요?

  • 그게 that (short for 그 것이)
    • Parallel with 이/그/저, there is also 이게 and, less commonly, 저게.
  • 무슨 what, what kind of
    • Contracts to 뭐
    • 무슨 is used more in formal writing as well as when used as a standalone noun.
  • 이다 to be
    • ant. 아니다 to not be
    • 이다 and 아니다 do not conjugate following the standard rules. Instead, in polite form, they become (이)예요 and 아니예요. In casual language, they are (이)야 and 이니야.

Caption: [처음 듣는 단어의 조합]

  • 처음 first
  • 듣다 to listen, hear
  • V~는/A~은
    • 는 attaches to verb stems to modify nouns.
    • ex. 제일 좋아하는 배우 누구야? Who's your favourite actor?
    • 은 attaches to an adjective stem to modify a noun, or to verbs to denote the past tense.
    • ex. 차가 작아요. The car is small.
    • ex. 작은 차가 있어요. I have a small car.
  • 단어 word, vocabulary
  • N~의 possessive particle
    • ~의 works very similarly to 's in English. It attaches to nouns to make them possessive.
